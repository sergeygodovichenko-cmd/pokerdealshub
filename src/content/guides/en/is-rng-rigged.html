📉 So If It’s Not Rigged, What Is It?

1. Variance

Even perfect play doesn’t protect you from losing streaks. It’s not a conspiracy — it’s statistics.

  • NL50 — typical variance ≈ 80 BB/100. Over 400 hands the standard deviation (σ) is √4 × 80 = 160 BB ≈ $80.
  • PLO5 50 — variance ≈ 175 BB/100. σ = √4 × 175 = 350 BB ≈ $175.

The chance of running worse than 1σ (−$80 at NL50) is about 16%. Running worse than 2σ (−$160 at NL50 or −$350 at PLO5) — around 2.5%. In other words, roughly one out of forty short sessions will feel like you’re cursed. That’s actually more likely than hitting a one-outer on the river — and we’ve all seen those happen plenty. In PLO, however, variance is naturally higher — swings can easily be 1.5 times stronger than in No-Limit Hold’em, so downswings there feel even more brutal despite perfectly fair randomness.

2. Selective Memory

Humans remember pain, not averages. Five brutal all-ins stick forever; five lucky ones vanish from memory. Without tracking software, our perception is always biased.

3. Rake Pressure

Now here’s a real enemy. Imagine you play NL50 with a solid winrate of +8 BB/100, but the room takes 10 BB/100 in rake. Your “real” winrate becomes −2 BB/100 — and suddenly it feels like the site is rigged against you, even though it’s just math.
