Rake is the commission that a poker room takes from every pot played. Usually it’s around 5%, and at first glance it seems insignificant. But the key point is that rake is charged every single time chips move from one player to another. Over hundreds of hands in a single session, even a winning player ends up losing a noticeable amount to rake.

Rake is the invisible tax on every move you make at the table. It turns poker from a game against other players into a game against the system — one where you must not only outplay your opponents but also overcome the constant drain of commissions.

How Rake Affects Winrate and Profit

Rake directly reduces a player’s winrate. You can consistently beat your opponents and have positive EV, yet still end up losing money at the end of the month — all because of rake. The higher the stakes and the more hands you play, the stronger the effect becomes.

Still, rake isn’t pure evil. Without it, the poker industry couldn’t exist: rake funds the rooms, tournaments, software, and security. It’s the necessary enemy that keeps the whole system alive.

If Rake Is the Enemy, Rakeback Is the Ally

Rakeback is the return of a portion of the rake you’ve paid. In other words, the poker room or club gives you back a percentage of the commission you’ve already generated. What started as a simple bonus has become one of the key tools for attracting and retaining players.

Without rakeback, it’s only possible to make money against the weakest opponents, and such tables are getting rare. That’s why rakeback has become a must-have for serious players and a pleasant, often significant bonus for recreational ones.

How to Get Rakeback
